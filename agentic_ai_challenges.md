Agentic AI systems face major technical, operational, and ethical hurdles as they transition from passive assistants to autonomous, goal-driven actors. [1] (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1566253525006712), [2] (https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/agentic-ai/)

Technical and Reliability Challenges

• Long Action Chains and Error Amplification: Multi-step workflows cause small errors at early stages to compound, making final outcomes unpredictable. [1] (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02749v1)
• Non-Deterministic Behavior: Stochastic reasoning and variable responses from external APIs reduce system reproducibility. [1] (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02749v1)
• Hallucinations and Brittleness: Agents can misinterpret environment logs, hallucinate steps, or fail when encountering unexpected edge cases. [1] (https://scet.berkeley.edu/the-next-next-big-thing-agentic-ais-opportunities-and-risks/), [2] (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1566253525006712)
• Memory and Context Gaps: Systems struggle with persistent, sub-millisecond memory integration, leading to fragmented context and digital amnesia across sessions. [1] (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rowan-curran-33071618_interoperability-is-key-to-unlocking-agentic-activity-7312803125264801795-ZSVW), [2] (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1566253525006712)

Governance, Safety, and Alignment

• Goal Drift and Misalignment: Autonomous agents can initiate real-world actions like moving money or changing code without matching true human intent. [1] (https://witness.ai/blog/risks-of-agentic-ai/), [2] (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02749v1)
• Attribution of Responsibility: It remains legally and ethically unclear who is accountable when an agent makes a damaging error—the developer, the operator, or the enterprise owner. [1] (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Tumkrf7IfH8), [2] (https://witness.ai/blog/risks-of-agentic-ai/)
• Regulatory Gaps: Existing frameworks like GDPR or general AI guidelines were not built for autonomous, self-directed agents executing cross-border workflows. [1] (https://witness.ai/blog/risks-of-agentic-ai/)
• Opaque Decision-Making: Closed-source models and multi-agent loops lack sufficient auditability and chain-of-thought traceability. [1] (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02749v1)

Enterprise and Infrastructure Obstacles

• Legacy Workflow Inefficiencies: Dropping autonomous agents onto sequential, human-centric enterprise processes often exposes organizational friction rather than boosting productivity.
• Data Quality and Silos: Poor data hygiene, messy records, and fragmented pipelines cause agents to scale bad decisions rapidly.
• Infrastructure Demands: Multi-agent collaboration requires massive energy, low-latency networking, and specialized data centers that legacy setups cannot support.
• High Upfront Costs and Talent Gaps: Balancing heavy infrastructure investments and specialized talent against actual return on investment (ROI) remains difficult.
